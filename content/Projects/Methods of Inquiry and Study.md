# Preamble 
There are a few general methods by which I like to approach studying new, particularly deep conceptual texts that require some time to understand.

This essay describes my study techniques. It proceeds on the analogy of a text, field, or network of concepts as a forest that I am tasked to navigate.

# “Flying above” 
>Also called “skimming” or “scanning”.

To get a “bird’s-eye” view of the main concepts of a text, I typically read it quickly and write [annotations](Annotation) as I read to make an initial map of the landscape. 

I typically don’t stop to do problems in this mode unless they’re quick or highly representative of an adjacent concept.

# “Chopping through trees”
>Also called [“deliberate practice”](<Deliberate Practice>).

To get a deeper, methodical understanding of a single concept, after a first-pass I go back and methodically work through problems involving my concepts-of-interest.

# “Observing a tree” 
Sometimes I like to stop and appreciate a tree. I tend to stop more often for weird, strange, difficult-to-understand trees. Trees that contain multitudes. 

My favorite trees that I like to stop and stare at for a while are tall, grand, extremely old, teeming with entire ecoystems in every fissure of bark.
