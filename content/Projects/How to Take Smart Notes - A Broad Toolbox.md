> Build a broad **latticework of mental models** for interpreting information in context. 

# Context
Experts recommend we learn by retrieval at irregular intervals and fact association - flash cards do this well. 

But the slip-box builds on this with a key advantage: **context**. 

Flash cards are atomic and isolated. Notes in the slip box are always embedded in one or more implicit contexts: problem settings, schemas, mental models. 

Beyond recall and associative mechanisms, **versatile mental models** are key to interpreting information. It’s good practice to cultivate and keep a broad toolbox of models so that you don’t get overly attached to a select, biased few. 

To implement this: 
- For each (powerful) discipline, seek out the most powerful central concepts in that discipline and understand it thoroughly such that it becomes a part of your thinking. 
- Then, interpret your experiences in terms of those mental models. *Never* “just” try to remember something.

`ext` This is why the oft-cited quote “learn from experience” is misleading - you don’t learn from just experiencing something. You learn from applying interpretive schemes to experiences, as well as from the experiences of others. 

# Effective Recall 
The basic principles of effective information recall are just what the slip-box implements: 
1. isolate the information you want to remember;
2. encode it by putting it into a note; 
3. practice recall via engaging with the box via pulling out and recombining notes regularly.
