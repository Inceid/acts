$$Paper = \text{rewrite}^n\text{(draft) = rewrite}^n\text{(arrange(permanent notes))}$$

A paper is many rewrites of a draft. A draft is a rearrangement of permanent notes. 

>"The slip box is an idea generator that develops in lock-step with your own intellectual development." (p. 75) 

It represents the history of your active attempts at translating works and theories in your own words. 

If done well, then writing a draft simply requires stringing together a logical, linear ordering of those translations and filling in missing connections.

To achieve this, always read and summarize literature keeping in mind possible connections to the slip-box [during](Syngenesis.md) *and* after your reading. 

The point eventually is to factor bibliographic/literature notes as support into idea notes for the main slip box.
