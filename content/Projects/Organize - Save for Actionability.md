##### `meta`
Notice that, while Forte has a good platform-agnostic framework for organizing encoded experiences from the world, there's substantial ambiguity to the organizational system *above* the PARA level.
Take this for instance: "PARA can be used across all the different places where you store information... for example, here is the documents folder on my computer \[and\] each of my active projects... inside these folders live the files that I use to *execute* each project." (p. 100)
So it's clear that the notetaking app is not used for information that executes the projects, but the computer is. What are the other relevant project functions that require separate platforms or separate compartments in a platform? Besides execution, there's long-term system integrity, long-term storage, backup storage, and other such systems that may require specialized formats or platforms. What seems to be missing is a broader operating system-like scheme for organizing experiences in general. PARA is nice and modular, but it doesn't seem to be able to encompass the actual system at work within Forte's own life, let alone my life.

I disagree with Forte's claim that the Second Brain needs to be as simple and easy to use as possible to enable optimal output. My Second Brain is simultaneously a productivity system, a reflective conduit, and an creative/artistic project in and of itself. I am completely okay with allowing it to be messy, dynamic, labyrinthine, perhaps even inefficient. It's beautiful. 
That being said, I understand that so long as I insist on this system containing my study notes and paradigms, principles of efficiency must operate over at least that area of the republic. 

Going forward, I will take steps to ensure that my projects and medicine-related materials adopt a more modular and action-oriented structure.
