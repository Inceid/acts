# Definition: IPs
**Intermediate Packets** (IPs) are the building blocks that make up your work, your corpus. They are *generalizable*, *project-agnostic*, *minimal* knowledge assets that you can use as the basic components to build out any new project you take on.
	 "Reusing intermediate packets of work frees up our attention for higher-order, more creative thinking."

There are generally five kinds of IPs:
1. Distilled Notes
	1. Books or articles you've read and distilled using Progressive Summarization.
2. Outtakes
	1. Material or ideas that didn't make it into past projects but could be used in future ones.
3. Work-in-process
	1. Documents, graphics, agendas, plans, or working code pieces you produced for past projects.
4. Final deliverables
	1. Concrete completed projects you've delivered as part of past projects.
5. Documents made by others.
	1. Any knowledge assets created by people you know.
	2. *Cite your sources!!*

# Definition: Retrieval Methods
**Retrieval methods** are methods to retrieve potentially relevant Intermediate Packets when we need them.
1. Search
	* Think of your Second Brain as a personal PubMed. You can run multiple searches or search strings to track down specific insights in your notes.
2. Browse
	* Working from broad to narrow, use your Second Brain's hierarchical/folder structure to gradually home in on your desired packet.
3. Tag
	* For IPs that defy easy spatial/hierarchical categorization, or for purposes of galvanizing distal cross-disciplinary connections, search through your notes by shared tag!
4. Serendipity
	* Sometimes a spontaneous connection arises. We can promote the probability of this happening a few ways:
		* Keep your focus broad while using methods 1-3.
		* Save and use images and visuals.
		* Share your ideas with other people, and pay attention to their reactions.
