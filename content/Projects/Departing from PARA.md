# Preamble 
[PARA](<Organize - PARA>) is a simple, effective framework for organizing the content for a “Second Brain” to optimize for simplicity and streamlining productive workflows. It consists of distinct folders for Projects, Areas (major areas of life responsibilities and topics of interest), Resources (a toolkit of ideas that we can plug and play into different projects), and Archive (all past projects). 

Three out of four of these folders are project-directed. As such, PARA is project-oriented: it takes externally directed packages of professional work as the primary object of value. 

This is a nice initial framework, but it doesn’t really work for me anymore. I don’t want a second brain for completing projects - nor a “second brain” at all. As its name and structure implies, I want `RI` to be a computational internal government. It will contain an object ontology for representing ideas and knowledge, a knowledge base, an operational semantics for manipulating and applying that knowledge base and my thought to navigating life, and other encoded representations from the world. But it’s not “my second brain”. I don’t need another brain in my life - I need accountability and systematicity. Too many projects, ideas, and dreams; too little structure, discipline, and accountability/governance of my time and resources. `RI` fixes that. 

Given these goals, the PARA framework is quite limiting. Projects are not the point of an internal government. Moreover, there’s no single point - a government must balance several values against one another to promote the well-functioning of its society: sovereignty, generativity, continuity, adaptability, democratic representation. We can’t necessarily have a folder dedicated to each major principle. But we can and should move beyond a project-centric organization. 

# Beyond PARA 
My reskin of PARA already does this to some extent: the External, Journal, Kernel, Projects, and Inbox framework nicely implements several important concepts:
- Distinguishing internal and external elements; 
- Distinguishing persistent state and government (Kernel) from execution (Projects);
- Distinguishing different forms of processsed state for notes (Inbox vs Journal vs Project);

Beyond this, each component has itself become rather well-developed. The Kernel has `RI`’s governance processes. Projects has all of my current, previous, and queued projects. External has the Embassy and contacts, the Medical Sector, captures, and other important areas of my life. 

Besides this, three items are in order.

## Resurrect Eidesis 
Eidesis, a garden of ideas, is something I’ve been trying to implement and automate for some time. It’s hard to automate idea synthesis. And the way I’ve sketched it, Eidesis is still far too vaguely defined. But I want to implement it over the next few years. So I’ve spawned a folder for it and reallocated a bunch of notes designated #fragment , #perennial , #dialant , and #vision into it. The hope is to eventually create a true agricultural pipeline for ideas within Eidesis. For now I will leave it wild and let ideas grow there, returning to nurture them once in a while.

## Move “register” notes to top-level
After working through [CS:APP](<Annotation of Computer Systems A Programmer's Perspective>), I’m realizing that the “hardware model” of `RI` includes a few notes that I access very frequently and hence need to be available outside of a folder. This includes a task [[Queue]] and the [[Atlas]]. So I’ve decided to tag them and keep them at the top for ease of access. [[Atlas]] will function as my main map of content for `RI`, so I’ll also heavily implement dataview queries as appropriate in it. [[Queue]] can be used for jotting down tasks quickly in Obsidian. 

## Stop expanding `RI`, start executing
`RI` is cool - it’s big, complex, weird. It’s enough. At some point I have to stop building the plane and start flying it. So I’ll slow down my structural expansions and direct my focus to my *actual projects*: 
- *Writing:* Essays, Annotations, Languages
- *Coding:* Programs, Apps
- *Portfolio Documents:* Syllabus Vitae, Curriculum Vitae Verus
