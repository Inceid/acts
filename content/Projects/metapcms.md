# Definition
A **metadegree** is an informal capstone curriculum that combines various elements from one’s formal curriculum with their natural interests for the purpose of pursuing a mastery of a specific intersection of multiple disciplines.

# Primordia
Meta-degree: PCMS (alias metapcms)
- lab notebook / daily journal and archive: `RI`
- progression map: Lucid
- worldly engagements - M2, AMA/MMS
- deliverables
	- I am the first and final deliverable

# Motivation
The motivation of a metadegree like mpcms is simultaneously that of a vast creative vision and a pragmatic/strategic imperative. 

The creative vision is to create a culminative form of syllabi, curricula, and deliverables for my personal lifelong education in the disciplines that resonate the most with me. 

The pragmatic/strategic imperative is based on a realization that (1) college was far overpriced compared to the value I got out of it, (2) there is much I wanted to learn that I didn’t get the opportunity to, and (3) I can make better curricular materials that are a better fit for my knowledge level than the vast majority of my professors ever could. 

# Resolutions
- To hijack institutional mechanisms as needed to fulfill this project’s goals. 
- To cast this as an intellectual project, as well as a practical, political, entrepreneurial one. 
- To abandon classical mechanisms of fulfilling my metadegree.

## Project Implementation
>The following describes the techniques I use at multiple levels to implement the major requirements of `metapcms`.

[[Ambient Monitoring]] and Persistent Reflection
*time: variable*
>I use ambient monitoring as a journaling and reflection technique throughout my day. 

Daily Problem-solving
*time: 10-30 minutes*
>I usually have a problem or exercise that I’m working on at any given point in time in one of the subjects I’m learning within metapcms. I can usually only handle a problem a day, occasionally two or three.

[[Rapid Research]]
*time: 20 minutes - 1 hout*
>*Rapid Research* describes techniques to turn my rapidly fluctuating interests into mini research projects. 
>Anytime I’m deeply interested in something, instead of watching a long video on it, I’ve gradually cultivated a habit of doing a mini-rapid-research project on it and writing up a report of my results.
