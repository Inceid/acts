Understanding the importance of cultivating deep work does not necessarily answer *how* we should go about executing depth in our daily lives. 

To this end, *The 4 Disciplines of Execution* by Harvard Business School professor Clayton Christensen provides a useful framework for implementing high-level strategies - originally written for businesses, but adaptable to our circumstances. 

# Focus on the Wildly Important 
Identify one or two "wildly important" simple, concrete goals or targets to prioritize above all others. 

>*Example:* I want to publish an op-ed on public health policy under the Trump Administration before the end of this year. 

# Act on the Lead Measures 
Lead measures are measures of performance or progress that depend on input effort or activity. Lag measures are measures of the thing you're trying to improve, and by contrast to lead measures, "lag behind" effort.

Lag measures typically measure outcomes of past performance, and hence usually materialize too late for you to change behavior in time. Focusing on lead measures redirects your attention towards what you can do to improve future performance. 

>*Example:* a lag measure of academic productivity is number of papers or citations. A lead measure is number of hours per week spent in deep work/research. 

# Keep a Compelling Scoreboard 
Keep a scoreboard of your performance on lead measures. 

>*Example:* how many hours of deep work am I putting in each week? 

# Create a Cadence of Accountability 
Establish a routinized accountability mechanism to track your lead measure scores and commit to practices to improving your scores.
