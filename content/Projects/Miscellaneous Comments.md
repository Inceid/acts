[[1]](https://d.docs.live.net/fe5d9419c2945bf4/Desktop/F2-3-8-18/philosophy/notes/sourcesOfNormativity.docx#_ftnref1) It seems that CMK’s bigger problem with this is that anything can be justified as a moral act under this consideration at a normative scale. But it shouldn’t be a problem if we have previously accepted a relativistic conception of morality. The person using his desires to justify his actions would not see any contradiction between his desires and others’ since he would not consider others’ desires in the first place. So this analysis doesn’t seem to work if we don’t already assume that a normative moral framework is possible.

[[2]](https://d.docs.live.net/fe5d9419c2945bf4/Desktop/F2-3-8-18/philosophy/notes/sourcesOfNormativity.docx#_ftnref2) CMK calls the point of view from which this objection arises the ‘moral sense’.

[[3]](https://d.docs.live.net/fe5d9419c2945bf4/Desktop/F2-3-8-18/philosophy/notes/sourcesOfNormativity.docx#_ftnref3) If this reading of Hume is accurate, then it makes sense where Kant comes in. He notes multiple times in his own Critique of Pure Reason that reason (of which the understanding is a subset) is a self-perpetuating force of the human will, and that its strength comes from its ability to self-criticize, an interpretation running completely contrary to that of Hume’s.
