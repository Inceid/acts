Classes teach writing linearly: *choose topic, gather reading, read, create ideas, write*. 
- But you can’t plan a topic before immersing in the literature; can’t read unless you are writing simultaneously; can’t squander in-the-moment epiphanies you have while reading. 
- Writing fueled by curiosity must guide the whole process. [We must write as we read](Syngenesis.md).

# How to start
- To find topics to write on, look at what you've already written on via note clusters. 
- Don’t brainstorm. **Brainstorming is what people who haven't been keeping track of their ideas externally via well-taken notes resort to. Bad tactic for projects.** 
- If a topic is uninteresting, move on. your notes will cluster around something else. 
`meta` even if you abandon a topic, leaving notes explaining why will yield insight!

# Advantages 
>"Following a circular approach allows you to implement many feedback loops, which give you the chance to improve your work *while you are working on it*... the ability to express (& verify) understanding in one's own words is a fundamental competency for everyone who writes..." (p. 54)
