# Preamble
Once you develop a method to encode, collect, and archive experiences, there’s a tendency to **hoard** them - to collect absolutely everything and keep it forever. 

This is a natural human tendency and is typically borne out of an attachment or deep love for the thing you’re collecting. 

But unfortunately, hoarding is generally qutie bad, both when it comes to objects and when it comes to collecting knowledge/experiences. It dilutes quality, eliminates intentional activity in selecting the experiences/objects to keep, and shifts the focus away from the collector to the object being collected (which is inappropriate). 

The solution is to apply a **curator**’s mindset towards the behavior: instead of collecting absolutely everything and saving it, ask yourself: “what are the highest-value, essential aspects of an experience or object I’d like to preserve? What *matters*?”, and optimize for encoding and collecting in that form. 

# Against materialism/commodification
A curator’s mindset protects against the worst urges and practices of materialism. 

A materialistic mindset towards collecting objects and experiences treats all such items as equal, and hence takes an *unquestioningly quantitative* approach to collection. A curatorial mindset treats certain items and aspects of experience as much more important than others according to certain pre-decided principles. 

Instead of mindlessly buying, collecting, archiving, and hoarding more of the same things, you carefully identify a set of principles and rules of thumb for keeping an item and use it to select anything new to add to your library or collection.
