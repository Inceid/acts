Proponents of open-office spaces and environments saturated with frequent socialization and (correspondingly) distraction from deep tasks often argue that, much like how random collisions between atoms can generate molecules of astonishing complexity and function, random collisions of ideas at a high-enough frequency between people of diverse backgrounds can reliably generate innovative breakthroughs. 

This is termed the *theory of serendipitous creativity*. It is often argued that the greatest centers of innovation modern innovation, specifically Bell Labs and MIT (after replacement of Building 20 with the new Stata Center), adopted this design in their architecture, having private/soundproofed offices connected by common hallways that forced intellectuals and experts from different backgrounds to interact frequently.
