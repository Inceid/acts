# Intuition via iteration
Key to a successful and virtuosic working relationship with the slip-box is the development of *intuition* - a feel for navigating ideas encoded into its formats. Intuition comes from long periods of dedicated, deliberate practice in wrestling with a problem and manipulating the devices used to dissect it.

`analog` A true laboratory is a site of experimentation with ideas. It has open-minded colleagues who freely discuss ideas and allow them to mingle, creating new ideas. 
`portal` [[1. The Argumentative Indian]], public reasoning & open dialogue 

Scientific innovation itself is more often the result of iterative incremental progress via small steps rather than sudden realization.

# Noticing small differences 
A similar principle of noticing *small* things is at work in the slip-box as well. Given visual exposure to packets of ideas in front of you, the ability to notice *small* differences between apparently similar ideas as well as *small* similarities between apparently differing ideas advances your work substantially. 

one advantage here of an analog (written) slip-box is that multiple notes can be splayed out and compared on a desk rather than one-by-one on a screen. 

Here, a proper use of comparison is key. **Comparison is our natural form of perception.**
