##### `pri`
* p. 115
	* put down your impressions on first reading - those are the initial instincts about what you thought was good or bad or didn't understand.
	* Coppola's model for distilling the *The Godfather* novel: break down each scene by synopsis, historical context, imagery and tone ("look and feel"), core intention, and potential pitfalls. Use keywords to evoke directorial actions or vibes, like "Frozen time" and "Hitchcock". Document your reactions *as you read and consume the content* so that your future self knows what stood out to you while you were reading it (kind of like we're doing now!) "The sheer amount of ink in the page would tell me later on this is one of the most important scenes". What is your analog of ink, then?
* p.118-119
	* *Discoverability* - the ease with which you can discover what a note contains and access its most useful insights - makes notes useful for the future.
	* To make a note discoverable, it must be distilled down to the key points and action steps. Get right to the point. Imagine your future self is president of a yuge company or hospital system. They're busy as fuck and don't want to listen to you drone on. How would you communicate your ideas and insights to them in the most concise way possible? Answer: **Progressive summarization.** 
		* Highlight the key points of what you're reading, then highlight the key points of those highlights.
* p. 124
	* add an executive summary to a progressive summary if you find yourself coming back to a note again and again. 
		* Use the bolds and highlights to write it. 
		* Use bullet points. 
		* Define terms in your own words. 
		* Summarize for a future self who needs to catch up to the note's significance and source's context *quickly*.
* p. 138
	* A helpful rule of thumb is that each layer of highlighting should include no more than 10-20% of the previous layer.
* p. 139
	* [[Stigmergy]] - each time you touch a note, make it more discoverable for yourself. Add a highlight, heading, bullet point, or commentary. Leave it better than you found it!
* p. 140
	* "Think of a storyteller who captivates you with every word. Their story is well distilled, with unnecessary details stripped away. Think about the last time you were entranced by a drawing or painting. Its ability to grab you immediately is a sign that the concept behind the artwork is compressed into its most compact form, allowing it to travel efficiently from the canvas straight into your brain."
		* `meta:` Disagree with this - stories that stick in your brain aren't always their most compact versions. Sometimes there are aesthetic flourishes and details included that promote their ability to bind our imagination. Distillation helps with understanding the underlying structure of something, but to say that every creative product resembles some minimal maximally efficient form of itself is erroneous.
* p. 141
	* "When the opportunity arises to do our best work, it's not the time to start reading books and doing research. You need that research to already be done." The best preparation for the challenges ahead is to exploit the effort you're already putting in, and staying curious about the world.
* p. 142
	* **Exercise:** find an interesting piece of content you've consumed recently, such as an article, audiobook, or video, and save only the best excerpts from it via copy-paste or capture tool.. *Italicize* the best parts. Then **bold** the best of the best parts. This comprises two distillation operations, which is enough for most use cases. See if you can recover the gist of the content from a glance at only the distilled note.
		* alternatively, capture, bold, then highlight. Distill in each step. Use the highlights as the pointers to the main idea of the note/capture.
