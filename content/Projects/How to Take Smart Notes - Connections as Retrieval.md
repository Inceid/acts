# Against perfect memory
Having a perfect memory is *not* a good thing. 

Russian journalist Solomon Shereshevsky, famous for being able to remember every detail of every experience he ever had, couldn’t forget anything even if he tried. 

Sounds nice, but it meant that he couldn't get the main point behind what he saw or read - details would involuntarily flood his mind. All he could do was repeat the content verbatim.

Our struggle with recalling information is a good thing: we suppress irrelevant information. But we can improve recall for learning by focusing on "retrieval strength" instead of "storage strength".
- Storage strength is the mind's ability to store new information. 
- Retrieval strength is its ability to recall that information given cues. 

# Learning as connection
In the slip-box, we promote learning by drawing meaningful **connections** between ideas, and connecting them to meaningful contexts. One idea or context can serve as a cue for another. 

Exploit variations and contrasts between notes. Imposing a too-rigid format can impede learning.
