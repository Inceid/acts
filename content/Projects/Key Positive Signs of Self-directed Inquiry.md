# Internal Reward Mechanisms
What gives you a sense of reward upon accomplishing something great? Is it other people's recognition of your accomplishment, or the mere fact that you accomplished it? Do you feel that you need other people to hear about what you did in order to feel good about it?

# Acceptance of Delayed Gratification
If the fact that a piece of work may not yield a tangible external reward for some time does not dissuade you from working on it, then you are more likely capable of accomplishing self-directed inquiries.

# Work in Silence and Solitude
If you're able to work for long periods of time without showing anyone your progress or talking to people about what you're doing, if you're able to work on something without getting paid for it or publishing it or seeking outside help/advice on it, and if you're able to keep your overall project a secret (except for the trusted people and/or collaborators whom you ask for feedback), then you are more likely than not capable of accomplishing self-directed inquiries. 

# Oppositional Action
Are you able to pursue a question even in the face of substantial opposition from people who normally support you? If you're able to stick to your guns and keep putting in work even with explicit discouragement from those around you or ridicule from those around you, then you are much more likely capable of self-directed inquiry. It's hard to act when the perceived majority of society or your surrounding society/network seems to be disinterested or against what you're doing. 
But if you're able to stay the course, that makes you all the more powerful.

# Self-Verification
In math, science, and typically secondary and higher education curricula, there's a distinct emphasis on correctness. Teachers and rubrics verify the correctness of your methods and work. To be able to complete self-directed inquiry, you have to become your own corrector, your own instructor. The faster and more rapidly/frequently you’re able to invoke self-verification and correction without becoming frustrated and exhausted, the more reliably you’ll be able to return to priority inquiries.

# i just can't get u outta my head, boi it's more than i want to think about 
If you find yourself coming back over and over again to the same questions and ideas, wondering and musing about them, dreaming about them, that's a sign that your mind is starving to conduct some kind of project on those questions. 

# Persistent Dissatisfaction with the answers you're given 
Perhaps it might be ideal to be motivated purely by positive emotions: curiosity, wonder, love, and joy. But it's equally important to understand and harness so-called "negative" emotions. A refusal to take box-standard answers as "enough".
