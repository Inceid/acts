# Preamble: From Unstructured Practice to Structured Practice 
You are already practicing all the things you want to do. You are practicing critical philosophical writing and commentary. You are also practicing programming and software design on a regular basis in some capacity. You’re studying and practicing policy writing. You’re actively creating a set of concepts for governing your life and thought. 

But the practice is unstructured and irregular. As a result, you don’t necessarily see an output from your efforts. Everything feels muddy and rushed. You feel somewhat at the whim of circumstance and your mood on any given day. This is a problem that must be solved if you want your practice to be effective and its effort not washed away over time. 

So your task for this year is to structure and regularize - to systematize - your practice. 

At the moment, it’s largely stimulus-driven, **distracted** practice. This is understandable, as medical school is constantly overloading you with erroneous and overwhelming stimuli. It is training you to become unfocused and undisciplined as a thinker, at least beyond its narrow cadre of disciplines. You have to fight back. You can fight back by instantiating deliberate practice. 

# Definition
**Practice** is the repetition of an activity under standard conditions. 

**Deliberate practice** is practice with full awareness, intention, and care towards every component of each repetition. 

`analog` Deliberate practice in the context of playing the violin involves practicing scales, arpeggios, and basic patterns before attempting difficult pieces. It distinguishes amateurs from professionals in this and many other fields. 
`pitfall` Self-deception
- self-deception through information overload; 
- self-deception through instant gratification

## Deliberate Practice is augmented by [[Devicism]]
I don’t have enough time to rapidly learn all of the things I want to learn. 

So it behooves me to come up with *devices* to learn the most important aspects of each field that I want to learn about. 

These are usually memorable mappings / memory aids, visual aids and reinforcement techniques, and specific abstract information structures that I use to organize facts so that they are easy to recall and associate. 

## Complex Practice 
Complex practice is practice that achieves multiple competencies at the same time. An example is manual notetaking on computer science and medicine on a laptop. This operationalizes many kinds of practice: typing quickly, creating and structuring notes, coming up with examples that you’ll read later, revising original written documents.
