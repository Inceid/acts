# Preamble 
Suppose you find yourself uncontrollably consuming content, be it video games, social media, movies, or anything else. 

You could spend months, years, decades of your life shaming yourself for an apparent lack of self-control, lack of discipline, lack of direction. In reality, the fact that you are able to expend so much energy on a small set of platforms is incredibly powerful: a certain platform has hijacked your motivational machinery and has bound it to scrolling and watching. *This energy can be re-hijacked*. It can be reclaimed and transferred to productive activities, activities that advance your life plans and make you stronger.

The purpose of this essay is to answer the question: in the era of internet media and perpetual consumerism, how do we systematically shift from consumption-based behaviors to productive behaviors? 

## Step 0: Mindset shift
The transition from consumption to production begins with a fundamental shift in mindset:

>Access to information is *not* your problem. 

You can find millions of book PDFs online if you look hard enough. You can find a Coursera course or MOOC on any topic you could ever dream of learning in college. You can learn about the history of any idea and event on Wikipedia. Access to knowledge, information, knowledgeable people, and the virtual simulacra thereof is not your problem. 

>Your problem is how to *make use* of information to achieve *your goals*. 

This requires an aggressive [Copernican turn](<The Copernican Turn.md>): a piece of writing, an idea, an object, has no intrinsic value on its own. To put it even more radically: factual pieces of information do not have any intrinsic value *even if they are true*. They have value insofar as they can be used to advance your highest goals and missions in your existence. 

If you convince yourself of this mindset, then what’s left is to determine what your highest goals and missions are; to write them down; to ask what actions they imply; and to get to work. 

>[!question] Why aren’t you telling me to produce something?
>
>The advice above does not tell you to produce anything. This is intentional. 
>
>The problem is not that you’re not producing anything: it’s that your mind and mindset is directed towards consumption. You can’t produce things even if I told you to. So don’t do it. 
>
>The first step is to mentally and emotionally rewire your mind so that production comes naturally to you. It shouldn’t be something you have to drag your feet to do.

## Step 1: Consumption to Curation 
[Curate content](<From hoarding to curation.md>) that resonates the most with you. This is largely intuitive: whatever resonates deeper than other things you’ve consumed is something you should keep some part of.

## Step 2: Curation to Exposition 
From curated content, create explanations in *your own terms*. 

I usually implement this via [annotations](<Annotation>) of things I’m reading.

## Step 3: Exposition to Presentation 
*Present* your work to others and get their feedback.

## Step 4: Start a Module or Project
If something maintains your interest after creating an exposition or presenting it informally/formally to others, consider starting a small [module](<Modules>) or project on it.

## Step 5: Iterate rapidly in cycles 
*Iterate* rapidly over projects on feedback you receive from others as well as your own opinions. 

## Step 6: Culminate 
After what feels like “enough” rounds of feedback, produce a [culminative form](<Culminative Forms>). 

A specific implementation is discussed in [[Ideation and Execution]].
