Writing a first draft *must* be separated physically and psychologically from proofreading, especially as they require different forms of attention that cannot coincide.
- Writing requires a floating sense of attention;
- Proofreading requires more focused attention.
- Combining and developing thoughts requires a more playful, open-ended sense of attention.
- Outlining requires a more broad-sense form of attention.
While writing, we want to free our brains from as much erroneous cognitive load as possible, including not having to think about the overall structure of our writing. As a general rule, to not think about it, have it in front of you (in the case of a paper, have an outline of the manuscript in front of you).

"Academic writing requires the whole spectrum of attention. To master the art of writing, we need to be able to apply whatever kind of attention and focus is needed." (63)

Not relentless focus, but *flexible* focus. "Successful problem solving may be a function of flexible strategy application in relation to task demands... The key to creativity is being able to switch between a wide-open, playful mind and a narrow analytical frame." (63)
