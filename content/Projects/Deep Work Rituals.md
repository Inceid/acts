>Rituals minimize the cost to transition one’s mind into deep thinking. 

Any effective ritual should address the following general questions.
1. *Location.* Where will you do the work? A location that is specifically dedicated to *only* deep work is best; otherwise you can transform your office/room into a context that is associated with deep work. 
2. *Rules.* What are the rules of the game? Are you allowed to use the internet or your phone? Establish metrics and concrete targets for your work periods. 
3. *Support.* What habits and activities will you use to support the work? For example, you can start the session with a cup of coffee or pepper it with 5-10 minute walks as breaks. You can organize your workspace to minimize transitional friction and context-switching. 

Answering all three of these questions serves to systematize your rituals. But really, any set of activities, analog, digital, or mental, that serves to systematize your process for enabling deep focus is a good set of activities to adopt and stick to.

If this set of rituals seems strange and objectionable to people around you, that’s for all the better. Your job is simply to find what sticks and enables you to focus and produce cool shit.
