# Two core abilities for thriving 
1. The ability to quickly learn hard/complicated things; 
2. The ability to produce at an elite level, in terms of both quality and speed. 

Both of these depend on your ability to perform deep work. 

Human beings are at their best when immersed deeply in something challenging. 

In *All Things Shining*, Dreyfus and Kelly argue that developments in Enlightenment philosophy that elevated the status of the autonomous individual over higher metaphysical entities / authorities, while it advanced rights and freedom for many, also led to a loss of a sense of "sacredness" thought essential to create meaning. 

The solution they suggest to the resulting nihilism is craftsmanship. They say that the task of a craftsman is not to generate meaning, but to cultivate his ability to discern and elucidate meanings already present in a rawer form. 

`meta` 
	The social manifestation of a craft into a lucrative endeavor is better called a *trade* - the craftsman can exchange his works to someone else for something else he needs to continue his work and life. 
	The evolution of a trade into a rule-governed set of activities to which other people might want to enter is better called a *profession*. What professions characteristically feature that trades may not are duties, gatekeeping, formalized training programs, licensure by the state. In short, a profession is an institutionalized trade. 
`return`

The meaning uncovered by craftsmen is not due to the outcomes of their work but by the skill, appreciation, and devotion inherent in the crafting.
