>[!info] What’s in the box!? 
>When deciding on what to write about, consult your slip-box for notes that cluster around topics. 
>
>This transforms your relationship to yourself and the box from **prescriptive** to **descriptive**: you answer the question “what should I write?” by asking “what’s in the box?”

# From top-down to bottom-up
The slip-box is an excellent system for simulating the selection and evolution of ideas: whatever ideas were truly interesting will have several outgoing notes and followups; the rest will stay isolated. Intimate familiarity with our prior ideas drives their evolution, as it motivates us to be playful with them.

# Follow your interests 
From its simplicity and flexibility, the slip-box promotes dynamic inquiry and fluctuating interests, allowing us to change course on projects. Our workflow should allow us to pursue the most promising leads, no matter how far they veer away from where we thought they would go.

# Finishing, Review, Modification
Some recommendations while completing the main product:
1. Structure your writing flexibly. Eliminate redundancies and nonessentials.
2. *Work on different projects at the same time.* There is generally a symbiotic effect among projects.
3. Instead of deleting elements from your writing that do not advance your argument/plot, move them to an overflow document for possible "later use".
