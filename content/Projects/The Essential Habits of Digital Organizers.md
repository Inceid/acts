##### `pri`
- p. 197
	- **Habits reduce cognitive load and free up mental capacity.**
- p. 198-221
	- **Mise-en-place** method: a set of small habits done *during the cooking process* that externalize standardized protocols for producing high-quality meals at a fast pace
	- For us as well, the only time we have available to maintain our systems is during the execution of our regular work.
	- Three "maintenance schedule" habits to ensure your Second Brain remains functional & relevant:
		- **1. Project checklists**: start and finish projects in a consistent way, making use of past work (hence creating a feedback loop that accelerates/efficiates/compounds rate of production from information).
			- Project Completion Checklist: *what can I learn from having completed this project for the future?*
				- **Mark** the project as complete
				- **Cross out** the associated project goal and move to "Completed" section. (the goals would usually be listed in a separate 'goals' note, sorted from short-term to long term)
				- **Review** IPs and move them to other folders.
				- **Move** project to archives.
				- **If project becoming inactive:** add current status note (a Hemingway Bridge for the next time you pick it back up) before archiving
				- optional inclusions: answer postmortem questions (what did you learn? what did you do well, what could you have done better? what can you improve for next time?); communicate with stakeholders; evaluate success criteria; officially close out the project and celebrate!
		- **2. Weekly & Monthly Reviews**
			- periodically review your work and life, decide on changes.
			- every week, clear your inbox, check your calendar, clear your desktop, clear notes inbox (at minimum, title, tag and file them), and choose tasks for the week.
			- every month, review and update goals (what you accomplished, what went unexpectedly), review and update project list, review areas of responsibility (and decide what you want to take action on in the coming month), review someday/maybe tasks, and reprioritize tasks.
		- **3. Noticing Habits**
			- notice small opportunities to edit, highlight, or move notes to improve their discoverability. organize as you go!
				- p. 218 -*notice when an IP that you already have could help someone else solve a problem, and share it with them even if it's not perfect.*
	- Project Kickoff Checklist: *what material do I have in my First & Second Brain to work with?*
		- **Capture** current thinking on the project as a brainstorm on a blank note.
			- Prompts: what do I already know about this project?
			- what don't I know that I need to find out?
			- what is my goal or intention?
			- who can I talk to who might have insights?
			- what can I read or listen to for relevant ideas?
		- **Review** folders or tags with relevant notes.
			- avoid tangents.
		- **Search** for related terms across all folders.
		- **Move** or tag relevant notes from the previous two steps to the project folder.
		- **Create** an Archipelago outline of collected notes and plan the project.
		- remember that you're making a plan for how to tackle the project. You're not executing the project itself *yet*.
		- optional inclusions: answer premortem questions like "what do you want to learn? what's the greatest source of uncertainty or most important question you want to answer? what is most likely to fail?"; communicate with stakeholders; define success criteria; have an official kickoff (schedule check-in calls, make a budget & timeline, write out goals & objectives to make sure everyone is informed/clear what is expected of them; can be useful even if a solo project!)
