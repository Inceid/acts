* p. 152
	* **Intermediate Packets** (IPs) are the building blocks that make up your work, your corpus. They are *generalizable*, *project-agnostic*, *minimal* knowledge assets that you can use as the basic components to build out any new project you take on.
	* "Reusing intermediate packets of work frees up our attention for higher-order, more creative thinking."
* p. 153-154
	* There are generally five kinds of IPs:
		1. Distilled Notes
			1. Books or articles you've read and distilled using Progressive Summarization.
		2. Outtakes
			1. Material or ideas that didn't make it into past projects but could be used in future ones.
		3. Work-in-process
			1. Documents, graphics, agendas, plans, or working code pieces you produced for past projects.
		4. Final deliverables
			1. Concrete completed projects you've delivered as part of past projects.
		5. Documents made by others.
			1. Any knowledge assets created by people you know.
			2. *Cite your sources!!*
* p. 154
	* Work on projects IP by IP rather than altogether in order to focus your attention on concrete items. 
	* Schedule time IP by IP rather than for hours of work on a single vague project item.
	* Get feedback IP by IP. People give better feedback if they're included early and if the work is clearly in progress.
* p. 160
	* Footnote: "Abstract thought can be difficult in and of itself, but fortunately it can often be mapped onto spatial thought in one way or another... spatial thinking can substitute for and scaffold abstract thought."
* p. 167
	* Part of the transformativity of IPs is that they are **easy to share** with others. Share your ideas often! Creativity is inherently collaborative.
* p. 171
	* Reading becomes easier and faster if you have a pre-existing purpose for what you're reading - if you know what you'll be creating from the reading.

* I'm noticing that one major difference between Forte's Second Brain model and `RI` is that `RI` is simultaneously a knowledge management tool for my creative and professional pursuits as well as a creative/therapeutic project in itself. Investing in expanding `RI`'s structure is both calming and provides an expressive conduit for me to build out my mind as I already see it work in the background of my consciousness. Rendering these processes conscious, I think, dramatically expands my processing powers in all sorts of ways. The upshot is that, whereas Forte's model is standardized and tuned to efficiency, I'm more inclined to accept a built-in inefficiency into `RI` for purposes of adding artistic spice.
