# Preamble
How do we build mental sharpness? How do we build a mind capable of holding and processing vast amounts of information? How do we build a mind capable of regulating itself to the effect of producing grand projects on scales never seen before?

Before anything else, you must have a desire to develop such a mind, and this desire must really be yours. It cannot be a desire on rent from someone else; it cannot be a desire forced upon you. It must emanate from your being and in a stable manner. It can't be something you just want today because a sharp mind seems fancy.

Once you have the desire, you cannot start building the mind yet. You must ask *why* you have the desire. Specifically, are there certain values or principles that inform this desire? From where do these principles originate, and how do you justify them? Articulate these principles as best as you can.

Now, once you have read the justifications for your desires, you may begin the logicostrategic part: to actually build out this mind and use it to shred concepts and problems like paper.

First, understand where you are. Second, understand where you want to go. Third, understand how to get there. Fourth, begin. Fifth, return.

# Understand where you are
My name is Suraj Joshi. I was born in India and moved to the US when I was 3. I grew up partly in Michigan, partly in Pennsylvania. I went to college at Carnegie Mellon University initially as a Biology Major, then I switched into Computational Biology. I also minored in Ethics. After graduating 1 semester late, I took 2.5 years off to do a Postbaccalaureate research fellowship at the NIH in Computational Cancer Genomics. Then I applied to medical school and was accepted to Tufts. 

I have a decent grasp of the structure of software programs. I know how to clean and analyze data, and I have a very good understanding of how to run Bioinformatics pipelines on computer clusters. I am passionate about population Bioethics and its subdivisions, especially health justice and fair health resource allocation during disasters. I love ideas, especially complicated, expressive ideas. I love language, mathematics, philosophy, and computer science because they are some of the only tools our species has discovered to express and behold the infinite. I also love people; I love talking to them, listening to them, and helping them. I love trying to apply what I love to medicine, because medicine is also a complicated and expressive idea.

# Understand where you want to go
I think I’m smart. I think I’m interested in important things. But I have noticeable lapses in my logical/mathematical thinking, fast information processing, and scientific reasoning abilities. 

I am also extremely vulnerable to social influence, adverse emotional experiences, and the desire for external validation. I also have a lot of trouble maintaining my motivation on projects - I find I get bored very easily and like to switch to other things. This understandably hinders my ability to effectively engage in long-term projects and generate an output from them.

## A theory
Before I die, I wish to develop a theory-of-everything. That term has lately been captured by physicists, but I don’t think a theory-of-everything belongs to physics. It belongs to every discipline. But, understanding that I can’t possibly become an expert in all discipline (nor do I want to), I’m happy to stick to what I consider foundational for explaining everything that matters and acting on it. 

As such, I want to develop a theory that encompasses the disciplines I think most naturally in and merges them into a common framework: computer science, medicine, analytical moral/political philosophy/ethics, and systems thinking. 

I want to use this framework to do research and come up with ideas that improve our social and technological systems to enable people to better live their lives and achieve their life plans. 

## A computer
I want my mind to be a supercomputer. I want to think, speak, and act with blinding speed and efficiency. I want to understand the ideas I talk about at an extreme level of depth. I want to be a brilliant thinker. But I don’t know how to do it.

`divinatio` `Sage` Assume you have already succeeded. What does a cognitive supercomputer look like? 

`Self` A cognitive supercomputer has astonishing facility with a diverse array of abstractions and languages to represent and manipulate those abstractions. Not necessarily someone who can speak in 15 human languages, but someone who operats as if they could - someone who deeply understands languages and uses that understanding to communicate effectively with human beings. 

A cognitive supercomputer solves difficult problems correctly and efficiently. 

A cognitive supercomputer enables higher-order mechanisms of thought and human expression.

# How to get there 
I don’t know how to get there. I haven’t found anyone who seems to know either. 

But my best guess at the moment is to figure out a few things that rouse me into a frenzied passion and optimize for them. The few things that I really, *really* love doing on a daily basis and rarely get bored of, I can find a way to max my time spent on those things and minimize the rest.
