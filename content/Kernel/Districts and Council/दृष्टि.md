# Preamble: Core Values
## Autonomy 
I want control over my time and activities. I want to work on my own projects for most if not all of my waking hours. 

## Generality 
I’m attracted to broad, general, abstract and analytical questions. 

## Large Systems
I love thinking about large systems and how to intervene on them. 

I most often think about and want to operate in technological systems and institutional/political systems. 

# Preamble: Second-Order Values 
## Foundation-building 
My vision of a physician is someone who builds foundational systems, or builds atop foundational systems. I have little to no interest in one-off case series-based clinical research. I have even less interest in highly technologically-dependent, flashy specialty-driven interventions that only serve to increase the divide between the privileged few who can afford the corresponding costly interventions and the rest of us. 

## Political Medicine 
[[Political Medicine]] is the generalization of social medicine in the setting politics and statecraft.

## Obsession 
Medicine as a field has increasingly selected for individuals capable of either being entirely engrossed in it or engrossed in a field complementary to it. Either way, engrossment - *obsession* - is a prerequisite. 

My vision of a physician is someone who is obsessed with morality, humanity, technology, but is able to harmonize those obsessions with the practice of medicine. The practice of medicine for me should comprise one part of my life - it should not define my life nor limit the other expertises and projects that I can pursue on my own time. 

## Integration 
There is no distinction between politics and medicine, nor between medicine and any other discipline.

My vision of a physician accepts no boundaries. I will accept no boundaries regarding the disciplines I allow myself to traverse in my initiatives to improve people’s health, wellbeing, and livelihoods. I will likewise accept no spatial boundaries regarding the places and organizations I allow myself to access in order to fulfill that vision. 

### Integration requires a tightly choreographed [Transition System](<Active Cycle.md>)
A transition system is defined as a set of distinct activity states augmented with a set of rules for transitioning from one activity state to another. 

If you want to do lots of different things in different contexts with any degree of success, you’re going to have to instantiate a transition system, a sequence or cycle of actions and engagements that rapidly pass control to one another until all tasks are done. 

## A Broad Scope of Practice 
It’s important for every physician to define in a robust and flexible/modifiable manner what the practice of medicine means to them. 

This is analogous to designing and structuring the scope and architecture of a software project or philosophical project. 

I want a broad scope of practice - not necessarily in the patients and conditions I see, but at least in the modalities in which I apply my mind to treat patients. I don’t care much for surgeries or procedures. I care a lot more about different styles of thinking; different flavors of care settings; different kinds of people to work with and take care of. Different things to do every day with my mind. 

## Purity and Pure Excellence 
As a medical student, resident, and physician, I will never produce low quality work. I will not produce garbage. I have a high standard as to the work I will produce that I would consider publishing or endorsing publicly. 

It’s worth seriously considering what *excellence* really means. Many people, upon serious reflection and construction of a personal conception of excellence, take a rather restrictive and narrow view of the concept. They say “I want my work to stand on its own merits! I don’t want my success to be determined by factors outside of my control.” Taken to an extreme, this belief set may preclude someone from doing all sorts of activities they deem erroneous or irrelevant to their conception of excellence that in actuality would help them achieve their conception. For example, someone who believes their resume and portfolio should stand on their own merits might think they don’t have to network with anyone or introduce themselves to other people. They might think that’s “gaming the system”, or somehow against the tenets of developing excellence in a craft. 

This is unnecessarily restrictive. Excellence is cultivated in myriad ways that we barely realize - one of those ways being by simply occupying the company of others who can feed into our efforts at excellence. That’s much easier if you know the right people. Knowing the right people requires seeking them out. It’s the height of conceit to assume that you don’t need anyone else’s company, guidance, acquaintance, or collaborative relationship to succeed. 

So a broader conception of excellence and the means to achieve it is, on balance, likely better as a life framework. 

### In thought 
My thought will be encyclopedic and capable of traversing distal realms immediately. 

### In rhetoric 
My rhetoric will be martial, muscular, incisive, penetrating, focused. 

# A collective psychology 
Some of the most powerful narratives and ways in which human beings have identified themselves in a hostile world and sometimes hostile society have been collective archetypes and abstractions. David and Goliath, Ram versus Ravana, the underdog. The Odyssey, Ramayana, the hero’s journey. The Iliad, Mahabarata, great wars. 

Archetypes and abstractions are *salient*. They stick. They are memorable. They resonate. All the above and more. 

One of my broader life goals, which I’m prototyping actively via [`RI`](<What is Republic Internum>) and the Council, is to create a collective psychological universe that can inhabit anyone who needs a mental home.
