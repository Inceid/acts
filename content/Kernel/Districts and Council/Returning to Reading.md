# Preamble
Reading is incredibly important. And I used to be able to do it incredibly fast and incredibly often. It's gotten much harder as I've gotten older, as my attention has waned and scattered to different aspects of life.

To return to reading consistently, start with a varied diet. You want to read many different kinds of things, and you want to start with things that are easy to digest, to get your system churning again. The key thing is to be able to complete interesting, digestible material and mentally reward yourself for doing so. That establishes the first iteration of a positive feedback loop to completing additional sections, books, or instances of whatever else you want to read. The hardest part of doing something is doing it the first time. Every single time afterwards becomes much easier, provided you remind yourself of this fact when you sit down to do it for the second time.

Eventually, as you read more and more material from increasingly diverse sources you will find that a voice awakens within you and begins to fight for its own spotlight, commenting on the work, suggesting its own ideas, asking its own questions. This isn't a distraction: this is a sign of a creative mind seeking an outlet. You've discovered a "mind's eye", rather a "mind's mind" -  an inner mind that occupies a mode of awareness of the present distinct from our passive conscious awareness of our environment. 

# The mind's eye
## Definition
The mind's eye is that aspect of the mind which embodies a distinct self-aware entity from the usual self. It can be thought of as a meta-self, or a reflection of the self looking from the back of one's mind out to the world. The mind's eye operates on a deeper set of rules than the conscious, commonsense mind. 

## General shapes
Don't spend time agonizing over every word. We don't parse ideas word by word. We parse by chunking into semantic categories: sentences, then subjects, verbs, and objects, among other things. Parse each paragraph into its main ideas. Do this slowly, in your own words. If a paragraph is taking too long, move on. Then come back to it. 

The mind's eye recognizes general shapes in the messages that it reads. It exploits context, environment, and peripheral details in assembling its understanding of the external (and internal) world. 

## Re-engagement patterns
If you find yourself reading a paragraph of a chapter or module and getting bored, skip to the end of the chapter to peek at the conclusion. Then work backwards by paragraph until you feel the need to return to your previous position. 

## Getting weird and gamification
We can read weird in many ways, and doing so makes it much more fun. Call these **gamification** approaches to reading. This is especially helpful for long, formal academic works and textbooks, which authors typically explicitly subdivide nicely into concept hierarchies that can be reorganized or approached as the reader sees fit, assuming conceptual dependencies in the book are mapped out. 
1. Read backwards by paragraph. This forces the mind to do several things at once. Given a paragraph, you have to infer what the previous paragraph must have been talking about, in addition to the rest of the context provided by earlier sections of a given chapter.
2. Read via **random walk** & **backtracking**. 
	1. Choose, in your mind, a random chapter to flip to after completing the first. 
	2. Read that chapter and try to understand as much as you can.
	3. For whatever concepts depend on earlier chapters, write them down. 
	4. Traverse backwards to those earlier sections. 
	5. Once you have terminated your backtracking, reconstruct the chapter from step 2 with the new knowledge.
	6. Choose another random chapter and repeat.
3. Read via **concurrency** and **parallelism**. 
	1. It's actually good practice to distribute your work across multiple projects at the same time. When one project bores you, move onto the next one. `portal:` [[Take Smart Notes - Six Steps to Successful Writing - 5]]

## Read with a light touch; scan for vibes
One of the biggest problems I have with reading nowadays is that I either get “stuck” or caught up on the same phrase, not properly understanding it for some reason, or I can skim multiple sentences without actually reading nor internalizing any of their meaning. 

On reflection, I find that a particular approach solves both issues at once and for formal and informal works alike, which is “reading with a light touch”. Another term for this is called “scanning”. The idea is to get the vibe of each chunk or unit of text. In the case of formal academic work, I read with a pen and paper in hand (or `RI` open in split-screen) and try to recreate what the author is saying in my own terms and diagrams. In the case of an informal, fictional, or for-fun work, I try to mentally recreate any images or scenes the author is trying to convey to me. But I try to do so quickly and move on, hence the “light touch”. 

The point of this is to subconsciously teach my mind that whatever I’m reading, it’s not going anywhere. I can pick it back up and reread the sentence later at any time - I don’t need to be able to interpret every point at the exact time that I read it. Hence I can scan approximate meanings of a sentence, paragraph, page, or chapter, write down what I understand, and, critically, *return to it later* if I missed some of the story or need a refresher.
