**Q:** What is the purpose of life? 

**A:** I'm happy to give my answer. The purpose of human life is creative rational activity. 

Creative activity is spontaneous, unsupervised thought and action that introduces new objects, methods, abilities, and realities. 

Rational activity is rule-governed thought and action congruent with the laws and behaviors of objects of representation. 

---

A byproduct of creative rational activity is that it promotes harmony among human beings and between humans and other living systems. But harmony is not the purpose of life. We can understand this by testing what happens when we seek it: if we strive too hard for it, we will likely fail to achieve it. 

That is because harmony *emerges* after some time when enough of us engage in creative rational activity. You cannot force it.

Sleep emerges after some time when we lay in bed after a day of exhaustion. You cannot force it. Harmony is the same.

---

Another byproduct of creative rational activity is that it promotes happiness, a state of contentment and fulfillment with oneself. But happiness is not the purpose of life. We can understand this by testing what happens when we seek it: if we strive too hard for it, we will likely fail to achieve it. 

That is because happiness *emerges* after some time when we engage in creative rational activity. You cannot force it.

---

Another byproduct of creative rational activity is that it promotes social status, respect, and prestige. But social status, respect, and prestige are not the purpose of life. This is a bit tricker: if we strive hard for it, we are likely to achieve it. But striving hard for social status, respect, and prestige for their own sake will more likely than not annul the other byproducts listed above. 

There are plenty of stores of those who chase status and prestige who find that they are stressed, unhappy, and in perpetual discord with the world around them. 

How can something we consider valuable which, upon its attainment, dominates, bleeds into, and sacrifices other values, be the purpose of life? We must look for something else. 

---

Human beings are differentiated from the other animals and living beings in their capacity to critically reflect, ask questions about their world, think rationally about it, imagine new realities, and create them. 

If we ask ourselves “What is my purpose? What is our purpose?”, we aren’t starting from scratch. A knife is good at cutting things. There are things we are *good* at, that we *naturally do*, that *almost all of us agree is good for us to do*, individually and in concert. It makes the most sense to generalize from that. It makes very little sense to sit around confused about what the “purpose” of our existence is when we already know what kinds of things we as beings are good it. 

Beyond vagaries like being kind, loving what you’re good at and getting good at what you love, obtaining skills, building a network, or any similar piece of cookiecutter advice we give young people, it helps to invoke a concrete unifying principle which at the same time allows for wide diversity and variability in how it is achieved. Different people may exercise valuable lives differently, but all purposeful lives, all lives well-lived, all good lives and great lives, share the same essential elements. 

The purpose of life is creative rational activity.
