# Definition 
**Decouple, Establish, Minlinking** (DEM) is a general strategy for promoting a robustly motivated but practically grounded sense of idealism in circumstances where my surrounding systems and institutions are not structured to promote my excellence or make me feel fulfilled. 

The procedure consists of three steps. 
1. *Decouple* your desires for excellence from their fulfillment in external engagements. Suspend at least for purposes of reflection any plans, commitments in the external world. 

	Another way to frame this is to decouple any action from exogenous incentives motivating that action. 

2. *Establish* private idealized, abstract mechanisms for fulfilling those excellences. 
	- a private mechanism is an activity you conceive of and do by yourself and iterate on via feedback with yourself. It may or may not also involve stages of exposure and feedback to others. 
	- private mechanisms also include plans, [core values](<Values Statement.pdf>), and [[Protective Abstractions]] for guarding against confusing or adverse experiences in the world. 
	
3. *Minlinking* once minimal viable versions of your private mechanisms have been established, create an injective mapping from your ideal system to any other activity you want to perform well. 
	- The purpose of minlinking is to synchronize one’s internal and external systems. Once private abstractions have been established, we can apply them to see how they perform in real-world actions. Where application is inefficient, we can modify the abstraction.

# Motivation
The premise of DEM is that for any given engagement, you may not find that the engagement satisfies all of your desires. And one of the worst things for a passion-oriented mind like yours is getting stuck doing something that you don’t desire to do. 

There’s an even deeper motivation to using the DEM framework to discover your most effective methods for pursuing your excellences. The idea is that all of us have certain abstractions that we use as controllers for navigating the world. 

`analog` Inverse myotatic reflex 
DEM serves as an autogenic inhibition reflex to overexertion, similarly to how the Golgi tendon reflex autogenically inhibits hypercontraction of its opposing muscle tendon. 

# Exact Implementation
We can start with a couple of examples immediately relevant to me. 

## DEM in Medicine 
### Learning Medicine
In the setting of my medical education, DEM is best understood as a method of casting or converting medical facts into a set of fundamental abstractions that best fits my [native vernaculars](<Vernacular Craft>), combined with a method of searching for and constructing a career that lets me invoke my favorite abstractions on a daily basis. 

In practice, the initial construction of this looks like elaborate diagrams, drawings, stories, narratives, anything that makes a concept stick for the long term. 

### Choosing a specialty
DEM in the setting of choosing a medical specialty requires:
1. D: decoupling all of your interests from medicine and medical specialties. This means, upon entering medical school,
	- Do **not** go asking residents what they did to get into residency at the prestigious program you think you want to go to;
	- Do **not** go around informationally interviewing people about the work they do and asking them how you can get to where they are;
	- Do **not** go around padding your resume with meaningless extracurriculars.
2. E: establish a basis for your interests. What physiological theories draw you in? What kinds of problems do you like to solve? What questions do you have about health and disease? What is your dream?
3. M: Upon discovering what your true interests and practices are *irrespective* of their manifestation in the theory and practice of medicine, you may then and only then minimally link your established abstractions and practices to the specialty that optimally matches. 

## DEM in Programming and Computation 
In programming and computation, DEM is best understood as a method of constructing my own theory and practice of designing computers and computational systems. 

In practice, this looks like learning fundamental low-level programming in an interest-based manner, then using what I learn to rapidly prototype simple personalized computer systems from scratch.

# Preserving the Integrity of a DEM System 
In my case, the outcome of a DEM is a set of [protective abstractions](<Protective Abstractions>) connected with highly valuable skills in my society. 

In the effort to preserve the integrity of such a system against threats and degradation, there are several relevant design considerations. 

## Immunity 
We say a DEM system is immune if it can maintain its integrity against questioning, challenge, doubt, or other form of resistance or damage from the external world. 

There are many ways to challenge a DEM system. One recurring challenge I encounter is the pressure to conform to the kinds of research other people are doing in medical school. I’m in medical school right now, and I feel pressured to be doing certain kinds of research that I’ve unfortunately simply lost interest in. I’m not particularly interested in small-scale quality improvement projects. I’m interested in big, fundamental / foundational styles of projects, projects that I can link across several disciplines, and projects that I can deeply personalize and make my own. 

There’s no shortage of people telling me that there are better things to do with my time than invest in myself and my systems. And I don’t care. I’d rather spend most of my time developing [[Decouple, Establish, Minlinking|DEM]] systems than anything else, as I’m convinced a robust set of internal systems will last a lifetime. 

### Don’t tell people about most of your work
`gen` Maintain a decoupled subsystem 

One way to establish abstractive immunity is to not expose most of the abstraction or its implementation to commentary or modification by the external world. In my case, this implies that it’s better for me not to release most of what I’m doing in `RI` or my research projects to other people. I’d rather not take half-baked advice and criticism from people who a) probably haven’t thought of something like this before and b) don’t have the patience to sit through my descriptions of the entire system to make an educated, nuanced recommendation for its development. 

The strategy this implies is to maintain decoupling between the work and any external incentive corresponding to it. The last thing I would want is to turn `RI`, *SS*, or any other platform in the system into a moneymaking machine. 

Not telling people about the bulk of this inner architecture and the process of expanding and refining it preserves [dominance](<Feedback Loops#Dominance>) of the feedback loop that drives creation. 

### Optimize minlinking for harmony 
As you minimally link a [[Decouple, Establish, Minlinking|DEM]] system to activities in the world, you want to maximize concordance between the internal motivation behind the activity and external motivations by parties to promote that kind of activity. You don’t want to minlink an action, such as typing updates into `RI` on your phone, with seeing a patient (for instance), because the patient or your supervising physician might be frustrated at your technology use. 

# Dangers 
Any DEM system, once it reaches a certain level of development, becomes extremely dangerous. 

DEM systems typically require a lot of authentic, vulnerable emotional investment. They come with certain expectations baked in. If you one day find a critical fault or hole in your system that needs repair, you'll feel the panic and distress that anyone feels when they encounter these problems in the real world. 

This is especially a problem with DEM systems because typically, nobody else understands quite what's wrong with you if you display any outward sign of depressed mood or hopelessness as a result of a problem in your DEM system. 

So for the most part, you have to deal with it alone. To the extent that you can communicate about problems with the system to others, you can only do so in broad strokes or after extensive analogy and conversion via adapters. In most cases, it ends up being more work than worth doing.
