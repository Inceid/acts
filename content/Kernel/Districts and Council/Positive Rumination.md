# Definition
**Positive Rumination** is a form of "ruminant" thinking, which is characterized by repeated and prolonged reflection on past thoughts and experiences, that helps one gradually make progress on solving long-term problems or establishing long-term systems. 

**inspo:** [pathoinversion](Pathoinversion.md) of depressive rumination

# Motivation
Too often retrospection is used to hurt oneself, to create regret where it need not exist. But retrospection can be *useful*. It can empower; it can suggest avenues for further exploration. 

Depressive rumination, fundamentally, is a [[Feedback Loops#Reinforcing Loops|reinforcing feedback loop]] of thought. It consists of negative thought that reinforces and reproduces itself. 

But alternative reinforcing loops are possible when that kind of thought is directed towards thinking about processes, problems to be solved, and systems to be improved. 

# Implementation 
`echo` [CPR](<Annotation of the Critique of Pure Reason>)

One of the best ways I’ve found to implement positive rumination is to take a large, complex book or work about a concept that deeply interests me and chew on it for several months. 

Thinking tends to form a positive feedback loop for me. The more time I spend thinking about a work, slowly digest its contents and assimilate them into my working schemas, the more I’m able to invest in doing that very thing, as opposed to thinking about my flaws or reasons why the world is falling apart.
