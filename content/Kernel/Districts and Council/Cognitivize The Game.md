# Preamble
I've always been obsessed with and loved playing video games. For so long, I wished life had background music, a soundtrack. I wished for so long that life actually was a video game. For so long, I thought that the worlds we imagined were better and more preferable to live a life worth living in than the world we lived in. I loved games so much I would spend until 2:00 am, 3:00 am playing them. This was true when I was a kid, a teenager, and is still true now that I'm an adult. I still stay up and play games like Rimworld, Stellaris, Hollow Knight, and others until well-past my bedtime. Why couldn't the real world be this exciting?

Recently (as of a few years ago), I've begun to change my mind - there are ways in which the real world can be made as exciting as the best possible game. I can engineer my life to have background music constantly playing around me. I can use my mind to augment life into something far more beautiful. But it's much easier said than done. And when I started to realize this, I didn't initially have the vocabulary to describe my solution nor the confidence in the solution to stick to it. 

But now I've discovered a perfect name for the trick: the trick is to Cognitivize The Game. What game? Pick your favorite game. Pick all the games you've played. They have pretty graphics and fast-paced combat, but that's not the essence of what makes a good game good, what makes an addicting game addicting. The essence of what makes a good game good is the feedback loop involved in the player's mind as they navigate the repetitive activities of the game. 

Cognitivizing a game consists of the same fundamental elements and activities as the cognitivization of any other activity. It begins with a straightforward mapping, choosing elements of the game to instantiate in your mind. You then play the game in your mind, animating the movements and events as vividly as you can. 

Eventually, you will find more efficient ways to animate a game than the game itself does. This is the beginning of [abstraction](<Abstraction>). The essence of the game plays itself out in your mind; the rest is unnecessary graphical artifact. 

The wonderful thing is that you don’t need to try too hard to do this. If you have any engagement with social media or the internet, your mind is somewhat accustomed to thinking in games.
