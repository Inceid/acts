# Preamble 
>How do we execute ideas?

`RI` is an ideation engine and reference tool. Housed in Obsidian in tightly connected markdown-style notes, it optimizes for adding new ideas and connecting them between each other. It also offers an easy format for storing and updating them throughout their life cycle. 

However, `RI` is *not* an execution engine. It is fairly difficult to linearize and prioritize tasks in Obsidian without extensive plugin work. And even then, doing so feels like it’s going against the grain of what Obsidian is optimized to be, which is an ideation, revision, and reference tool. 

I have lots of ideas. And I want a good chunk of those ideas to be implemented soon. So I need to establish a pipeline for rapidly implementing any idea that sticks around in my mind. 

# An Implementation Pipeline
The following comprises a generalized pipeline to take an idea in whatever stage it presents, [capture](<Thought Capture>) it for storage and later retrieval, and transform it into progressively refined implementations in the real world in the form of projects or states in `RI`. 

## 1. Log and reflect
A sufficiently well-formed idea should be logged into `RI`.

Otherwise, the idea may require some more refining and deliberation. This is best done with pen and paper. 
>Do this in *Selfstudy* or a [Reflectorate](<The Reflectorate>) notebook where appropriate. 

Both options require a bit of time to enfleshen the thought. If you don't have time, it suffices to spawn a note, tag it as an #eidesis note, and send it to `Eidesis/` for later processing. 

## 2. Detach 
Separate yourself from the idea for at least a day. 

Let it cook. If it resurfaces, it’s worth pursuing. If it doesn’t, it’s probably better left in Eidesis until you come back to it naturally. 

## 3. Allocate
Decide where the idea belongs. 

The basic division here is inside, outside, or at the boundary of `RI`. 
1. Is it a persistent concept that should be part of the "standard library" comprising `RI`? Then it is destined to live in `RI`. 
2. Or is it better suited to serve as a project? Then it's destined to live outside of `RI`. 
3. The last option is that it exists at the boundary mediating between `RI` and the world. This is usually labeled as an [adapter](<Adapters>). 

If you choose the idea to be a project, decide on an [output type](<Project Types>) for the project, rank a project priority, and follow [[Project Design Theory|proj]] to see it through. 

## 4. Finish
If the idea is a [concept](<Concepts>), complete the following steps upon finishing it. 
1. Subtype it: is it primitive, derived, or higher-order? 
2. Integrate it into the existing concept hierarchy. 

If the idea is a [project](<Project Types>), release it to the world as its type specifies. 

Finally, if the idea is an [adapter](Adapters), test it out. Adapters generally function as translators between one part of `RI` and another *or* between `RI` and the external world. So test it. Does it translate data well? If not, tune it to be better. 

## 5. Produce a culminative form 
A [**culminative form**](<Culminative Forms.md>) is a summative, final, highest form of a project. 

# Appendix: Ideas that are too hard 
Some ideas are too hard to implement at my current level of expertise. My default solution to this has been to shore up my background via reading textbooks & doing their exercises as well as implementing prototypes of ideas to test my understanding of them. 

All progress on expanding my background knowledge is documented in successive versions of the [[Syllabus Vitae 02.27.2025.pdf]].
