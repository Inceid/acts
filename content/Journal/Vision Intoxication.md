# Definition
**Vision Intoxication** is the process by which a mind becomes distracted or misdirected by the novelty, stimulation, or inspiration of one of its visions or dreams. 

Reflection on the coolness of a task or beauty of a dream can, paradoxically, paralyze or slow the mind from acting. 

# Motivation 
Generally speaking, vision intoxication is bad. Getting drunk on your dreams is nice when you’ve earned the reward of accomplishing them and celebrating, but if the passion to a dream *inhibits* progressive action towards accomplishing that dream, we need to examine the cognitive/emotional processes that stunt our ability to act on our dreams.

# Tactics
## Overclocking 
Insofar as a vision casts to an addictive stimulus, [[Overclocking]] that stimulus can help. The idea is this:
1. You desensitize to the stimulus.
2. Desensitization lets you ask “what did I just do?”. It allows your rational mind to briefly regain control and appraise the situation.
3. Then you can make a decision about whether to keep doing the thing.

## [Become a curator](<From hoarding to curation>)
Love is dangerous. If you love something so much you become addicted to it, then without commensurate restrictions on your behavior you will destroy yourself in pursuit of the thing.

Curation can protect against this. I *love* video essays. I watched them constantly in college - they likely literally kept me alive. But I recognized that this love was becoming an addiction when I started studying for medical school and tried to focus on research. To stave it off, I decided to take agency over the rising addiction and curate video essays into playlists and write reflections on them in my notebooks.

You can (at least theoretically) apply this to anything else. Drink a lot of alcohol and want to drink less? Become more discerning about the alcohol you drink. When you drink, taste. Let it linger. When you taste different drinks, ask “what’s the difference between these? Which one do I like more, and why?” 

Externally, it may not look like this is doing much. But by asking discerning, detailed questions about the thing you’re addicted to, you slowly develop a sense of agency over it.

## Become a scientist
Curation is fertile ground to develop a science. Once you learn the habit of observing your reactions and experiences, you can start writing them down. Enough of a sample size of observations and you have a dataset. Enough datasets and you have a research project, which can become a science.

Alternatively, you can turn to commentary or critique. Observe your response to the pleasurable item; if it sucks ass, write down *exactly why*. Use as visceral, flowery language as you can. Keep writing this down over time. Then, go back and analyze your prior reactions. What patterns do you respond well to or less well to, and why? 

As you do this consistently, you begin to develop a working theory of yourself. This is essential to making any decisions in an informed manner.
