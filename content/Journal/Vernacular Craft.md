# Definition
**Vernacular craft** is the practice of assembling creative products from ordinary materials into one's own vernacular or using the resources around oneself. 

Noticeable especially when using resources not specifically intended for such an assemblage. Ex. a dress made of forks.

Vernacular craft encourages reverse engineering things and rebuilding them from scratch. 


`inspo:` I remember peeking through this book of Afrofuturistic art from the Harvard bookstore, and it was so beautiful. It was beautiful specifically because so much of the art in that book was created from ordinary, everyday materials like foil, forks, and pieces of fabric. But these materials were combined in a way such that the final product assumed an aesthetic status far greater than the sum of its bare parts. *That* is vernacular craft. The practice of creating beauty by improvisation on one's current environment and materials. Not waiting for the right moment to strike or the right sketchbook and set of pencils to appear, but creating right there in the moment.
Vernacular craft is utterly natural in children. They make up exciting, hilarious games. But we are taught to silence those instincts.
