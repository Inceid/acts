# Definition
**Subjective computation** is a form of computation that manipulates the bare primitives of one’s mind, desires, and base-level attitudes about the world. 

It is distinct from objective computation, which manipulates external objects (usually, data or bits on a computer) or abstractions of objects.

# Implementation
A key constraint of any model of Subjective Computation is that, whereas objective models make use of primitive operations on physical chips or circuits, subjective models make use of the primitive operations of the mind. 

In my models, I assume that these primitive operations can be freely chosen, so long as the resultant set of primitives is nonempty. 

My attempt at implementing Subjective Computation is via [Kernel operators](<Operator>). [[The Kernel]] is the “subjective computer” of my mind and its operators specify rules for manipulating my thought. 

Rules are decided based on what is fun and efficient. I’m most likely to use rules that are fun to use; I’m also most likely to succeed at thinking using rules that are efficient to implement and effective at producing their intended outcomes.
